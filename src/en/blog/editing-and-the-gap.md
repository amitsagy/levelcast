An episode can sound great on headphones and still send your hand to the knob the moment you play it in the car. That doesn't mean the editor didn't do the job. It means there are two different volume problems, and usually only one of them gets solved.

## The level of an episode

The first problem is that different episodes arrive at different levels. One show was recorded loud, another quiet, and someone listening back to back doesn't want to readjust every time a new episode starts.

There's a familiar fix for that: normalisation. You measure the episode's average level and move the whole thing up or down until it reaches a fixed target. Many editors do this before uploading, and plenty of players do it themselves. After normalisation, one episode won't be several times louder than the next.

## The level of a conversation

The second problem lives inside the episode. A podcast with two people has two voices, and each came from a different recording: a different microphone, a different distance from it, a different room, and sometimes a remote line that already squeezed the voice on the way.

Normalisation doesn't touch this. It moves the whole episode together, so the gap between host and guest passes through normalisation exactly as it was. If the guest was quieter than the host before, they're quieter after. The episode as a whole is simply at the right level now.

Closing that gap means handling each voice on its own. Big productions do it in the edit, track by track. In many small productions, where the same person records, edits and uploads, that's the step that gets left out. The episode sounds good, the average is right, and the conversation still rises and falls.

## Why it's the car

At home, on headphones, your brain compensates. Even a relatively quiet voice sounds clear, because nothing competes with it.

In the car there's constant noise: engine, tyres, wind. To hear the quiet voice over it, you turn it up. Then, when the loud voice comes back, it's much louder than it needs to be. A gap that was mildly annoying at home becomes, on the road, a reason to touch the knob every few minutes.

## What a listener can do

Not much. You can pick a compromise volume and strain a little whenever the guest talks. You can keep your hand near the knob.
